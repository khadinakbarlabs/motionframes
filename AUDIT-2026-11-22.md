# toonshorts audit, 22 Nov 2026

> Written when the project was called **toonshorts**; it is now **MotionFrames** (same engine, same commands under the new name).

What I did: copied `forge-test/` (the bridge-gaps short) to a temp folder, deleted the generated files
(word timings, sound lock, downloaded sounds, `index.html`, renders) and ran every step of toonshorts 0.2.0
the way a new user would, timing each one. Then I looked at frames from the render, from `v1.mp4` and from
the final `pip-short.mp4`.

Machine: macOS, node 22, ffmpeg 8, HyperFrames 0.9.74 via npx.

`make` stops at the first failure, so on a fresh project with a flaky network a new user never gets a video.
When every step works, the run is about 3 minutes from spec to checked mp4. The hand-written scene files take
far longer than that.

## Problems ranked by impact

### 1. Picture: characters are small, stiff and thin (biggest problem)
- Pip is about 440 px tall in a 1920 px frame and stands far from camera in every scene. On a phone he is
  a small figure in a big empty frame (see `docs/media/before-closeups.jpg`).
- Limbs are single 11-12 px lines with no outline, so he reads as a stick figure in a bean shirt. Hands
  are missing. Feet are flat black ovals.
- The face has two dot eyes and a 16 px mouth. There are no brows, no pupils, no expressions: the
  rig cannot look surprised, worried or proud. Lip sync is one open/closed flap per word.
- No shading, so nothing reads as 2.5D. The only depth cue is the drop-shadow ellipse.
- The walk swings the legs from the hip with no knee or foot plant, and the arms do not move in the
  idle pose. When Pip is not walking he stands still apart from blinks.

### 2. Backgrounds: flat and rebuilt by hand in every scene
- Each scene redraws the sky, sun, clouds, hills and water from scratch (s1, s2 and s6 copy the same
  40 lines). There are no parallax layers: the camera creep scales the whole picture as one flat card.
- No foreground layer, no atmospheric haze, no light. The "3 depth layers" rule in `motion.md` is not
  supported by the engine, so it is not followed.

### 3. Camera and transitions: basic
- Every scene gets the same linear 1.04x scale creep (`ease: "none"`), so motion feels mechanical.
- Cuts are hard cuts or a 0.4 s cross-fade. There is no whip, push-through or wipe, and the cut whoosh
  is placed on the cut frame without a matching picture move.
- The `fx.kick` shake moves whole scenes in 1-frame steps; it reads as a glitch more than an impact.

### 4. Captions: right size, wrong words
- 90 captions of 1-2 words, but the grouping is mechanical, so groups end on filler: "GAPS IN",
  "THAT SLIDE", "EXCEPT HIS", "VERY FIRST", "A PHONE", "NOW INSPECTS". The strong word often sits
  alone in the next group.
- Every caption has the same pop. Hot words only change colour. No emphasis scale, no tilt, no shadow.
- Scene text such as "JULY" and "35°" competes with captions near the end (the check warns about it).

### 5. Process: fragile and hand-heavy
- Sound search depends on Openverse being up. It timed out during the audit, and media-use then
  refused to download a "similar" sound. The engine throws on the first failure and writes nothing, so all
  earlier downloads are lost from the lock.
- No shared sound cache: every new project re-downloads "whoosh" and "pop".
- Errors hide their cause: `words` printed only "Command failed" (the real cause was npm's cache being
  read-only in the sandbox). `check` with no `index.html` printed "FAILED, 0 error(s)". `build` with no
  word timings printed `word "steel" not found in L3 ()` instead of "run words first".
- There is no single command from spec to finished mp4: `make` stops before render, and `render` is a
  separate step with 648 lines of output (27 raw JSON trace lines). That output cannot be recorded for the
  tutorial.
- Every scene needs about 40-80 hand-written lines of SVG and GSAP. Breathing, blinking and talking have to
  be wired by hand for every character in every scene, and they are easy to forget (s2 and s6 have no
  breathing, and nothing turns the head between beats).
- Stills are separate, and there is no preview of one frame per scene.

### 6. Sound placement
- Cut whooshes peak exactly on the cut frame and do not follow the picture move.
- Caption pops are added on every hot word, even when a manual cue already plays at that moment.
- No ducking: the river bed and long cues sit at the same level under the voice.

### 7. Small bugs seen in frames
- s2: the splash drops are visible from the start of the scene, because the `fromTo(... opacity: 1 ...)`
  tween renders its start state before it begins (frame at 9 s: drops under the gap before the phone falls).
- v1 (`levels/lv0`) had sentence captions, dark empty frames and dead air. 0.2.0 fixed the captions
  and dead air, but the final short was improved mostly by hand-building scenes rather than by the engine.

## What "10x better" has to mean here

1. A character that reads at phone size: bigger, outlined, shaded, with hands, brows, pupils, 5 mouth
   shapes and named expressions, alive by default.
2. Ready-made layered backdrops with real parallax, so a scene is a few lines instead of 40.
3. Eased camera moves per scene, and designed transitions with sound that follows the move.
4. Captions that group on meaning and punch the key word.
5. One command (`toonshorts go`) from spec to checked mp4: resilient sound finding with a shared cache,
   clear progress, step timings, real error causes, a preview contact sheet before the render.
